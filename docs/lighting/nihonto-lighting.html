Nihonto reflection geometry

An adjustable geometric model, not a prediction of hamon contrast or a calibrated photograph. All distances are in millimeters.

Two separate choices: framing sets camera distance; local surface tilt sets the light angle. Low side lighting can reveal scattered detail while missing the smooth face's mirror reflection entirely.

Cross-section controls

 

The tubes are symmetric, parallel to the blade length, and 1219.2 mm (48 in) long. Height h is above the selected blade-center surface, not above the table. Offset d is from the blade centerline, not from its edge.

Frame / stitch planner

u is measured from the front principal plane. H is measured from the entrance pupil. These are not generally the same place. Sensor distance u + v is only a thin-lens estimate; lens-front working distance requires the exact lens. Measure framing with a ruler at blade height.

Rose = tube visible in ideal reflection. Background = neither tube visible. A dark result does not mean the real blade will be black: scattering, ambient light, surface texture and polish are deliberately omitted.

Each point uses its own viewing direction and actual tilted-plane height. The tubes are finite closed cylinders, modeled as emitting over their entire surfaces. This is a geometric map in blade coordinates, stretched to fit the panel, not a perspective camera render.

No lens-barrel or stand occlusion, finite aperture, emission-angle limits, diffraction, Fresnel weighting, roughness, scratches, or measured blade profile. One uniformly tilted plane cannot model a real sword's multiple facets and curvature. The two sides of an actual blade need not have symmetric normals.

The math

At the blade center, the camera is directly above. Let alpha be the local normal tilt toward the right tube, measured from vertical. The surface itself has the same tilt magnitude relative to horizontal.

theta = atan2(d, h);   alpha = theta / 2;   h = d / tan(2 alpha)

For a horizontal mirror surface and a right tube center at (d, h), with camera at (0, H):

x = H d / (H + h)

This is the reflection point for an ideal line-source center in the same cross-section. It must lie on the blade. At the exact center of a flat face, a side tube never supplies the direct specular ray unless its emitting area reaches the vertical direction.

General 3D form, with vectors pointing away from surface point P:

v = normalize(C - P)
n = normalize(l + v)
q = 2 dot(n, v) n - v

The ray P + t q, for positive t, must intersect an emitting surface. The simulation tests intersections with the actual finite cylinders. For tube radius r at center distance R, the central cross-section normal half-width is asin(r/R)/2.

Camera and stitching

m = sensorSide / F
u = f (1 + 1/m)
v_image = f (1 + m)
step = F (1 - overlap)
N = 1 + ceil(max(0, B - F) / step)

Here m is positive magnification magnitude. Sensor size is 35.9 x 23.9 mm for Nikon Z5II in FX mode. Camera pupil height in the ray model remains independently adjustable because focal length alone does not determine it.

Suggested experiment, not a universal optimum

Start with one tube at d = 80 mm, sweep h through 20, 100, 250 and 450 mm, and compare the same patch at fixed exposure. These select central normals around 38, 19, 9 and 5 degrees. Then add the second tube only if it improves the features you want. Maximum mirror brightness need not maximize detail contrast.

For stitching, translate without changing camera height or orientation; lock exposure, focus and white balance. Compare overlapping details before a full run. Changed viewing directions can change highlights even when illumination is unchanged.
